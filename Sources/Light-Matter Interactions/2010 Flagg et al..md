## Key Arguments

> ([[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=3&selection=20,0,25,1&color=important|p.3]])
> Despite the non-negligible differences between the QDs in coherence time, lifetime, and charge state, their photons still interfere. 

> ([[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=4&selection=129,0,139,1&color=important|p.4]])
> The indistinguishability of two photons is a property independent of the measuring device. Therefore, while a cw photon source could be utilized in an application requiring indistinguishable photons, postselection will be required if controlling the photon arrival time is necessary. However, to accurately characterize the indistinguishability an unconditional measurement must be performed. 

## Key Results

![[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=3&rect=313,68,567,454&color=important|p.3]]

The [[Quantum Dots]] is based on InAs using [[Distributed Bragg Reflector Cavity]] [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=1&selection=89,0,114,49&color=yellow|p.1]].

The spectrum of the [[Quantum Dots]] are tuned to match with piezoelectruc transducer [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=1&selection=118,0,122,1&color=yellow|p.1]].

The time resolution of the the SiAPD is 640 ps which is close to the coherences time of the QDs involved, making it hard to recover the interference [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=1&selection=152,0,156,49&color=yellow|p.1]] [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=2&selection=138,37,198,1&color=yellow|p.2]].

The coherence time of the QDs are too short compared to its lifetime which suggest that it is susceptible to environment noise [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=2&selection=210,11,253,31&color=yellow|p.2]].

Each QD's single photon purity is verified with [[Hanbury Brown and Twiss (HBT)]] setup to be over 90% pure [[2010 - Flagg et al. - Interference of Single Photons from Two Separate Semiconductor Quantum Dots.pdf#page=2&selection=254,0,277,43&color=yellow|p.2]].
