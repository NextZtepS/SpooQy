## Key Arguments

A direct absorption, along side HOM and Bell Measurement, can used to transfer the quantum state from the emitting matter to the absorbing matter [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=1&selection=148,0,155,89&color=important|p.1]] [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=2&selection=0,0,3,14&color=important|p.2]].

Ab [[SPDC]] source in designed to match the spectral and temporal profile on the InAs/GaAs [[Quantum Dots]] to let the photons interact with the QDs via direct excitation process [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=2&selection=41,7,45,18&color=yellow|p.2]]. **Read more on this!**

The QD's trion state is the target for spin-photon entanglement with the SPDC [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=7&selection=0,17,5,79&color=yellow|p.7]].

> ([[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=10&selection=3,3,7,85&color=important|p.10]])
>  The current work could be immediately extended to show a heralded transfer of a photonic qubit generated from the frequency modulated cavity-SPDC photons using an external modulator [55] to the spin ground states of a charge QD through a direct absorption measurement [12,14]. In addition, such photonic qubits could be teleported to the spin state of a single QD by interfering it with the photons of a spin-photon entangled state [56].


## Key Results

The pump (470.98 nm) is generated via SHG for the pp[[Potassium Titanyl Phosphate (KTP)|KTP]] with type-I interaction, 50 ps pulse with 78 MHz repitition rate [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=2&selection=50,0,81,78&color=yellow|p.2]].

Monolithic cavity is used by coating one side of the KTP crystal [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=2&selection=83,0,85,75&color=yellow|p.2]].

The measured bandwidth of the [[SPDC]] signal and ilders are 610 GHz compared ot the theretical prediction at 540 GHz [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=4&selection=121,28,122,49&color=yellow|p.4]].

KTP is tuned to get a double-resonance (degeneraate) for signals and idlers + QD's photons; addtionaly 6 GHz atalon ([[Fabry-Perot Cavity]]) filter is used [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=4&selection=139,21,144,21&color=yellow|p.4]].

The [[SPDC]]'s single photon property is verified using [[Hanbury Brown and Twiss (HBT)]] setup up to 65 mW before the higher order multi-photon emission rates take over [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=6&selection=84,19,117,49&color=yellow|p.6]].

[[Quantum Dots]] is housed in a another [[Distributed Bragg Reflector Cavity]] (which helps with collection of the emission) [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=7&selection=7,3,9,49&color=yellow|p.7]].

A trion state has a selective transition with $\sigma^+$ (R) and  $\sigma^-$  (L) polarized photons. If we only send in H and collecting V coming out, we can probe the excitation of the trion state [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=7&selection=55,65,66,98&color=yellow|p.7]]. 
Input H is on resonant with the QD for possible interaction to create R or L coming out in which are able to pass through V-Pol. If there is no interaction, nothing should come out as H can't pass through V. [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=9&selection=38,53,43,53&color=yellow|p.9]]

A tunable CW laser is used to probe the profile of the QD's photon and replaced afterward with the SPDC photons [[2019 - Paudel et al. - Direct excitation of a single quantum dot with cavity-SPDC photons.pdf#page=9&selection=32,0,34,28&color=yellow|p.9]].
